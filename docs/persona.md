# Priority — User Personas

> ⚠️ These personas are **hypothetical archetypes**, not validated through research. They are intended to guide design decisions until real user interviews (20–30 students) are conducted.

---

## Persona 1: Priya — The Overloaded Pre-Med

### Demographics
- **Age:** 20
- **Year:** Sophomore
- **Major:** Biology (Pre-Med track)
- **Location:** Large state university, lives in a shared apartment off-campus
- **Budget:** Tight — part-time campus job, parental support for tuition only

### Lifestyle Snapshot
- Sleeps 4–6 hours on weekdays, crashes for 10+ hours on weekends.
- Skips breakfast, eats dining-hall food at inconsistent times.
- Walks across campus but no structured exercise.
- High academic stress: organic chemistry, volunteer hours, MCAT prep.
- Drinks 2–3 cups of coffee, minimal water.
- Social life is limited; feels isolated but too busy to address it.

### Relationship with Health
- **Knows** she should sleep more and eat better. Doesn't need education — needs prioritisation.
- Has tried fitness apps before; uninstalled within a week because "they wanted me to log everything."
- Distrusts anything that sounds like it's diagnosing her — she hears enough medical language in class.
- Would respond well to: "You've slept under 5 hours three nights in a row. Tonight, try setting a hard stop at 11:30 PM."

### What She Needs from Priority
- **Speed:** Check-in must be < 30 seconds. She will not open the app if it feels like a chore.
- **One clear action:** Not five suggestions. One thing she can realistically do today.
- **No guilt:** She already feels behind. The app must never make her feel worse.
- **Trust:** If it says something, she wants to understand *why* — not a black-box "AI says so."

### Potential Friction Points
- May forget to check in on extremely busy days.
- May under-report stress because she normalises it.
- May dismiss recommendations she's "already heard" (e.g., "drink more water") unless framed with personal context.

## Persona 2: Marcus — The Social Night Owl

### Demographics
- **Age:** 22
- **Year:** Senior
- **Major:** Communications
- **Location:** Mid-size university, lives in a house with four roommates
- **Budget:** Moderate — works part-time at a local restaurant (evening shifts)

### Lifestyle Snapshot
- Sleeps at 2–3 AM most nights (mix of socialising, late shifts, and phone scrolling).
- Eats irregularly: skips meals, then orders delivery at midnight.
- Plays pickup basketball 1–2 times per week, but inconsistently.
- Moderate stress: graduation approaching, job search anxiety, relationship strain.
- Drinks alcohol socially 2–3 times per week; drinks minimal water.
- Feels physically "off" but can't articulate what's wrong.

### Relationship with Health
- **Interested but passive.** Would like to feel better but won't make it a project.
- Never used a health app seriously. Downloaded one once for a New Year's resolution; lasted two days.
- Responds to concrete, relatable framing — not clinical language.
- Would respond well to: "Your sleep timing has shifted 3+ hours this week. One thing that could help: no screens after 1 AM tonight."

### What He Needs from Priority
- **Low commitment:** If the app asks for more than a minute, he's out.
- **Relevance:** Recommendations must feel like they're about *his* life, not a generic wellness pamphlet.
- **No judgment about lifestyle:** He's not going to stop going out. The app should work with his reality, not against it.
- **Weekly perspective:** Might not check in daily, but would look at a weekly summary if it's easy to digest.

### Potential Friction Points
- Inconsistent check-ins — may have gaps of 2–3 days.
- May not take the app seriously if recommendations feel generic.
- May be uncomfortable reporting alcohol consumption or stress details.
- Evening work shifts create non-standard schedules that don't fit "normal" health advice.

## How These Personas Inform Design

| Design Decision | Priya Signal | Marcus Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Check-in must be ≤30 seconds | ✅ Will abandon if slower | ✅ Will never start if slower |
| One priority, not a dashboard | ✅ Needs focus amid overload | ✅ Won't process multiple metrics |
| Personal baseline, not population norms | ✅ Her "normal" is unusual | ✅ His schedule invalidates standard benchmarks |
| Non-judgmental tone | ✅ Already self-critical | ✅ Defensive about lifestyle choices |
| Weekly Insights only with sufficient data | — | ✅ Gaps mean data may be sparse |
| Safety layer for red flags | ✅ Chronic sleep deprivation risk | ✅ Alcohol-related patterns |
| Plain-language explanations | ✅ Ironically, wants *less* clinical language | ✅ Won't engage with jargon |

---

*These personas are design tools, not validated user research. They should be revised after conducting structured interviews with real students.*
